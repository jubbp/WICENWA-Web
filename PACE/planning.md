This chapter is how WICEN WA works out what it can offer **before anyone deploys** — for an emergency activation and a planned event alike. For an activation it starts when WICEN goes On Watch or a request arrives; for an event, when the organiser first asks. It ends with an answer to the requester, a briefed team and a roster.

Every tool here works on paper. The coordinators' Field Guide carries all of them, with the member list already printed on the availability tally, so the plan still works when phones and internet don't.

Response planning is not yet adopted as a procedure {% include gap.html id="GAP-062" %}.

## Who plans

| Role | Does |
|---|---|
| **State Coordinator** | Owns the decision, talks to the requester, and appoints the Response Planner. For a small task the State Coordinator may do the planning themselves. |
| **Response Planner** | Appointed by the State Coordinator for each activation or event. **The contact point** for members' availability replies and planning questions, and named in the call-out. Keeps the intake record, sizes the task, keeps the availability tally and drafts the roster. |
| **WICEN Incident Coordinator** | Once WICEN is activated, runs WICEN's part of the incident ([Activation](./activation)). May be the same person as the Response Planner. |

## Never overcommit

WICEN WA offers only what it can staff safely and sustain usefully. A smaller offer that is kept is worth more to an agency than a larger one that breaks down on the second day.

A **minimum response standard** — the least WICEN will deploy, for safety and to be useful — is still to be set {% include gap.html id="GAP-063" %}. Draft points for the committee:

- **Safety:** nobody works alone in an emergency area; check-ins on the agreed schedule; operators rested and within shift and rest limits; a safety briefing and the agency's induction before starting.
- **Usefulness:** every deployed station has a working link back to the net, with a fallback tier; WICEN can sustain the task for the agreed minimum period, relief included; kit and power last the shift.

Whether WICEN offers *part* of a request is also still to be decided. Until it is, the State Coordinator tells the requester plainly what WICEN can and can't provide, and never promises more.

## The steps

1. **Take the request** — the intake record (below). Confirm it is genuine: call back on a number you already know for that agency or organiser {% include gap.html id="GAP-018" %}.
2. **Size the task** — which response packages, how many positions and shifts, relief and travel.
3. **Call out for availability** — on every channel at once, replies to the Response Planner.
4. **Tally the replies** — one sheet, whatever channel each reply came in on.
5. **Decide** — what's needed against who's available, and the minimum response standard.
6. **Answer the requester** — what WICEN can provide, from when, for how long, and what it can't.
7. **Plan and brief** — the communications plan, the briefing, and the first roster. Plan the end from the start ([Demobilisation](./demobilisation)).
8. **Keep it current** — tally again each day or shift; tell the requester early if WICEN's capacity drops.

For an event the same steps run weeks ahead, as part of the [Event Support](./event-support) engagement lifecycle.

## 3. Sizing the task

Rules of thumb:

- **One position around the clock** is three 8-hour shifts. With rest limits and someone unwell or delayed, plan **four operators** per 24-hour position.
- **Field sites are staffed in pairs.**
- **Multi-day tasks:** add a relief rotation, and travel time counts towards each person's day.
- Start with the first 24 hours, then the first 72.

## 4. Availability call-out

Send the call-out **on every channel at once** — internet messaging, the email list, SMS — and call it on the watch net {% include gap.html id="GAP-040" %}. Everyone replies to the Response Planner, by any channel.

> **WICEN ON WATCH** — *[incident or event]*, *[area]*. We may be asked for *[package(s)]* from *[time]* for *[duration]*. **Do not deploy.** Reply to *[Response Planner: name, call sign, phone]* by *[time]*: **AVAIL**, **LIMITED** or **NOT** — from when, until when, where you are now, kit (VHF, HF, Winlink, 4WD), and whether you can stay overnight or several days.

*Example reply:* "AVAIL from 0600 Tuesday to 1800 Wednesday, Bunbury, VHF HF Winlink 4WD, multi-day yes."

**When the internet is down,** Net Control calls each member in turn on the watch net and takes the same answers. Members who haven't replied by the deadline get a phone call if there's time; otherwise they're counted as not available.

Replies are personal information: the tally is held by the Response Planner, used only for the activation or event, and kept with the restricted records afterwards.

## 8. Briefing — SMEACS

The briefing order Australian emergency services use (AIIMS). Brief every station before it deploys; at events, with race control and key event staff.

| | Cover |
|---|---|
| **S**ituation | What has happened or is planned; the area; weather and fire danger; who is in charge |
| **M**ission | What WICEN is providing, for whom, and until when |
| **E**xecution | Sites and stations, who goes where, nets and frequencies (the communications plan), timings |
| **A**dministration and logistics | Travel, access, food, water, power, shelter, relief and shift times, sign-in and sign-out |
| **C**ommand and communications | Who WICEN reports to; the WICEN Incident Coordinator; Net Control; check-ins; position reporting |
| **S**afety | Hazards from the Activity Safety Assessment, lone working, fatigue, the exit route, NO DUFF, how to stop |
